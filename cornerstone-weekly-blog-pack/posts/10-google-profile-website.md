## Article body

A customer finds your business on Google Maps, checks the reviews and taps through to your website. The profile says you handle weekend appointments. The website says Monday to Friday. Before anyone calls, there is already a question about which information to trust.

**The short answer:** your Google Business Profile helps eligible businesses appear with useful local information; your website gives customers the detail and contact journey you control. Give each a clear role and keep shared facts consistent. Treat them as connected parts of the same customer experience.

## What should your Google profile do?

Help someone identify the right business and check the basics: what you do, where you serve, when you operate and how to contact you. Use the real business name and appropriate categories, with accurate contact details and current photographs.

Google's guidelines require businesses to represent themselves accurately and use a precise address or service area. Eligibility depends on the business model; a profile is not automatically available to every online-only operation. [Source: Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).

A profile can answer an immediate question, such as whether you are open. It has less room to explain a complicated service or help someone compare several project options.

## What should the website do?

Give the customer enough detail to make the next decision. A service page can explain the work included, relevant experience, process, likely requirements and what happens after an enquiry.

For a hypothetical landscaping business, the profile might establish the business and operating area. The website can distinguish a garden redesign from routine maintenance, explain the site-visit process and show genuine examples of each type of work.

That distinction helps people ask for the right service. It also gives you a useful destination when someone needs more than an opening time or phone number.

## Decide which information belongs where

| Customer question | Profile's role | Website's role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this the right business? | Accurate name, category and contact details | Clear identity, service descriptions and genuine work |
| Do they cover my area? | Accurate location or service area | Practical coverage, exclusions and travel considerations |
| What does this service involve? | A concise overview | A fuller explanation of scope and process |
| How do I take the next step? | Relevant contact or website action | A working enquiry, booking or purchase journey |

You do not need identical wording everywhere. You do need the underlying facts to agree.

## Follow the journey from the profile

Open your profile as a customer and follow its website link. Is the destination useful? Can someone recognise the business, find the service they came for and make contact on a phone?

A general homepage may work for a broad business introduction. Where a specific action or service link is available, use a destination that matches it. Check the final page and form, not just whether the URL opens.

Then try the phone route. A tap on a number is a different outcome from a call being answered. Make sure your team knows what happens when a call arrives during a visit or outside normal hours.

## Keep changes connected

Write a short update checklist for changes to your phone number, services, opening hours or coverage. Assign someone to update the website and relevant profiles together.

Consider a change to Saturday appointments. The website, booking calendar, profile and phone message may all need attention. Updating only one creates conflicting expectations. Give temporary information a review date so it does not become permanent by accident.

## Measure outcomes from both places

Review profile activity alongside website enquiries and confirmed customer outcomes. Different reporting tools may count different actions, so define what each number means before combining them.

Where appropriate, a tagged website link can help identify profile visits in analytics. It will not reveal every part of a customer's research. Ask how the customer found you and record a useful answer alongside the enquiry.

Start with the service you most want to sell. Check its profile information, website explanation and contact route from beginning to end. Fix conflicting facts and unnecessary steps before adding more content.
