## Article body

Your business serves Brentwood, Billericay and several surrounding towns. Should the website contain a page for every place, or one page explaining the whole service area? The answer depends on what each page would help a customer understand.

**The short answer:** create a separate location page when it gives customers useful, accurate information about the service in that area. If the only difference is the town name, improve the existing coverage page first. A location page needs a purpose beyond repeating a search phrase.

## Distinguish a service area from an office

A business can travel to customers without having premises in every town it serves. Explain where you are based and where you work. Avoid presenting a coverage area as a staffed branch.

Google's business-profile guidelines require accurate representation of addresses and service areas. A website location page does not create eligibility for another business profile or an office that does not exist. [Source: Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).

A clear statement such as “Based in Brentwood, serving customers across the surrounding area” can be more useful than a set of invented office descriptions.

## Ask what changes for the customer

A separate page may be justified by a genuine difference in the service, access arrangements, availability or the type of work you undertake there. Relevant completed projects can also provide useful local context.

For a hypothetical garden-design business, one area may contain several completed courtyard projects with particular access considerations. That creates something specific to explain. Simply changing “garden design in Brentwood” to another town name does not.

## Choose the right page structure

| Situation | Sensible starting point |
| --- | --- |
| One service delivered consistently across a small area | A strong service page with clear coverage |
| Several services and a broad operating area | Service pages plus a useful coverage overview |
| A genuine local difference or substantial relevant project evidence | Consider a dedicated location page |
| Many near-identical pages with little useful detail | Review, consolidate or improve them |

This is an editorial decision, not a fixed quota of pages every business needs.

## Include information that helps someone buy

Explain which services are available in the area and any practical limits. Describe the first step, the information you need and how appointments or visits are arranged. Publish lead times only when you can keep them current.

Where you have permission, include real local work and explain the relevant challenge. A short, accurate example can give a customer more confidence than several paragraphs praising the town.

If travel charges or access requirements affect the quote, explain the process. Do not invent local pricing bands solely to make a page appear different.

## Use genuine local evidence

A project photograph should show the work described. A customer quote should be authentic and used appropriately. Avoid attaching the same review to several locations in a way that suggests several different customers.

If you have no relevant local project yet, describe your actual coverage and service process honestly. You can add more specific evidence after completing work. A useful page does not need fabricated proof.

## Avoid pages created only to funnel visitors elsewhere

Google describes doorway abuse as pages created to rank for similar queries and lead people to a less useful intermediate destination. Its examples include regional or city-targeted pages that funnel users to one page. [Source: Google Search Central spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

That does not mean all location pages are prohibited. It means the page should genuinely serve the visitor's task. Keep it useful in its own right and provide a clear, relevant next step.

## Connect the pages sensibly

Link from the coverage overview to the pages that exist, and connect local information with the relevant service. Use labels that explain the destination.

Avoid an enormous footer list that exists only to repeat location keywords. Review whether a customer can navigate the coverage naturally. Check the phone number and enquiry destination on every page.

## Review what the pages actually achieve

Look at the searches bringing people to each page, the information they use and the suitability of their enquiries. Keep availability and coverage current as the business changes.
