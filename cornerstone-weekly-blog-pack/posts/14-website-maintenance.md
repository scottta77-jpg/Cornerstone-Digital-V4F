## Article body

The website is live and the final invoice is paid. A month later, an enquiry form stops delivering, an old staff member still has access and nobody is certain which account renews the domain.

**The short answer:** after launch, your website needs clear ownership, reliable recovery arrangements, appropriate technical upkeep and regular checks of its customer journeys. Hosting provides somewhere for it to run. Maintenance and improvement are separate responsibilities that should be described in your agreement.

## Separate the services in the proposal

A single monthly payment can cover several different things. Ask the supplier to explain them in plain terms so you know what is included.

| Service | Typical purpose | Useful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting or platform subscription | Keeps the site running on its chosen infrastructure | Who controls the account and renewals? |
| Maintenance | Handles agreed technical upkeep and checks | Which tasks, systems and response arrangements are included? |
| Content support | Makes agreed changes to information and assets | How much work is included and what is excluded? |
| Growth work | Tests and improves visibility or customer journeys | What outcomes and deliverables are being reviewed? |

The actual scope depends on the platform and contract. Do not assume each label includes everything in the table.

## Keep control of the important accounts

Know where the domain is registered, how hosting is paid for and who has administrator access. Include relevant analytics, form-delivery and booking accounts in the handover.

Use appropriate access controls and remove access when people no longer need it. Recovery details should belong to the business or be held through a clearly agreed arrangement. A forgotten personal email account can become a practical barrier when something needs changing.

## Ask how recovery works

Find out what is backed up, how often, where it is stored and how it can be restored. A site export, a database backup and a record of customer enquiries may be different things.

The NCSC includes backing up important information in its guidance for small organisations. Your website arrangements should support the business's wider recovery needs. [Source: NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security/backing-up-your-data).

Ask for an appropriate restoration check. A backup that exists but has never been tested leaves an important question unanswered.

## Match technical upkeep to the platform

A self-managed content system may require software and extension updates. A hosted platform may manage parts of the infrastructure while your team still owns content, integrations, account access and configuration.

Ask what is your responsibility and what the platform handles. Review changes in a suitable environment when needed, keep a record and check essential tasks afterwards. “The provider handles security” is not a complete description of every business responsibility.

## Check the routes customers rely on

Periodically submit a controlled form test, check receipt and confirm the customer acknowledgement. Open the booking calendar, follow important links and review key pages on a phone.

A useful check follows the customer task rather than only checking whether the homepage is online. An external widget or form service can fail while the rest of the site continues to load normally.

## Keep information current

Review services, prices where published, opening hours, contact details and staff information. Remove expired offers and old booking instructions. Give seasonal notices an end date.

Check images, documents and links that may have changed elsewhere. Keeping the information trustworthy is an ongoing task even when the underlying website code needs few changes.

## Define how support works

Agree how to report a problem, which issues are covered, who assesses urgency and what response times mean. A response acknowledgement and a completed fix are different commitments.

Clarify the difference between a defect, a small content edit and a new feature. If you want a new booking process, that may be a project rather than routine maintenance. Understanding the boundary makes the arrangement easier to manage.

## Review evidence of the work

Ask for a concise record of checks, changes and issues that need a decision. A maintenance report should explain what was done and what remains, rather than simply presenting a score.
