## Article body

Your team closes for a few days over Christmas, but the calendar still offers appointments. The website promises a reply within one working day. An automated message says someone will call shortly. Each system appears to work, but together they promise something the business cannot deliver.

**The short answer:** treat holiday availability as one connected update. Confirm what the business can provide, reflect it across every customer contact point and test the complete booking and enquiry journey. Make clear when a person will respond and when normal service resumes.

## Agree the operating plan first

Write down which dates you are open, which services remain available and who is covering enquiries. Separate the hours customers can visit from the hours someone monitors messages or delivers a service.

For a hypothetical maintenance business, planned visits may pause while an agreed urgent-response service continues. The customer needs that distinction, including any limits. “Open over Christmas” may be too vague to explain what is available.

Assign one owner to coordinate the updates and record when temporary information should be removed.

## Check every place a customer sees availability

| Contact point | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Website and service pages | Clear holiday dates and relevant service limits |
| Google Business Profile | Accurate special hours or other appropriate status |
| Booking calendar | Closures, reduced capacity and available appointment types |
| Form acknowledgements | A realistic date or timeframe for a human response |
| Phone and voicemail | Current hours and an appropriate alternative route |
| AI or chat tools | The same approved availability information and handover rules |

Include any other channel your customers actually use. One accurate notice cannot correct a conflicting promise elsewhere.

## Update the Google profile appropriately

Google provides special hours for short-term adjustments and closures of up to six consecutive days. Its guidance recommends confirming holiday hours even when they match normal hours. [Source: Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en-GB).

For a longer closure or a different operating arrangement, check the current guidance for the appropriate profile setting rather than forcing every situation into special hours. Then review what a customer can actually see.

## Test the calendar's behaviour

Block dates when appointments cannot take place and review reduced staffing or different appointment types. Check the latest bookable slot before closure and the first one after reopening.

Try rescheduling an existing booking as well as making a new one. Confirmations and reminders should reflect the actual appointment, not an old default. If an external calendar manages availability, check that the website is using the updated information.

## Make acknowledgements useful

A receipt can confirm that an enquiry has arrived while explaining when it will be reviewed. Give a specific, accurate reopening date or response expectation where you can.

For illustration: “Thanks for your enquiry. Our office is closed until [reopening date]. We will review new requests when the team returns. Existing customers needing the agreed out-of-hours service should use [approved contact route].”

Replace every bracketed detail with real information before using the message. Do not describe an inbox as monitored or a service as urgent-response unless the arrangement exists.

## Update automated tools as well as the website

Review the information used by your chatbot, AI receptionist and follow-up workflows. A customer should not receive one answer from the page and a different answer from the assistant.

Decide which automations should pause. A sequence chasing a quote may need different timing if the business cannot answer the reply. Keep genuine service communications useful and avoid sending requests that create work nobody is covering.

## Make the notice easy to use

Put the essential information where someone making contact will see it. Keep the wording short and connect it with the affected action. A booking-page notice should explain the effect on bookings.

Check the mobile layout and keyboard access. A large pop-up that hides the enquiry form can create another obstacle. Give temporary notices a clear removal or review date.

## Run one complete customer test

Follow the journey from Google or a service page through the calendar or form to the confirmation. Check the destination inbox and the information the team receives.
