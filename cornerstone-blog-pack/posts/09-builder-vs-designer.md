## Article body

A website builder gives you tools to create a site. A web designer provides a service that may include planning, content, design and implementation. These choices overlap: a professional can use a website builder, and a custom-coded site still needs a clear brief.

**The short answer:** choose an approach around the work required and who will do it. A straightforward site can suit a capable owner using a builder. A more involved customer journey may justify professional help. The platform name alone does not establish quality, value or suitability.

## Start with the customer task

Write down what visitors need to do. Read about your service and send an enquiry? Compare packages and book a consultation? Place a complex order? Those tasks create different requirements.

Then identify what your team can provide. Copywriting, image selection, page structure, form testing and keeping information current all need an owner. A tool can make parts easier without removing those jobs.

## When doing it yourself can make sense

A builder can be a practical option when the site is relatively simple, you have time to learn the tool and you are comfortable managing the content. You can make routine changes directly and work at your own pace.

The trade-off is your time and responsibility for decisions. Before starting, test the difficult part of your requirements, such as a booking rule or enquiry destination. Avoid choosing solely because a sample homepage looks attractive.

## When a web designer adds value

Professional help is useful when you need someone to turn an unclear brief into a coherent site, write or organise the content, establish a consistent design and test the customer journey.

Ask which of those jobs are included. Some designers focus on visual work; others provide strategy, implementation and support. The label is less useful than the deliverables, examples and responsibilities in the proposal.

## When more custom development is justified

A tailored implementation may be appropriate when your process requires unusual interactions, specific system connections or behaviour that an available platform cannot support sensibly.

That needs a clear specification, maintenance plan and handover. More custom work creates more choices to manage. It is worth doing when it solves an identified requirement and the business can support it after launch.

## Compare the approaches consistently

| Question | Build it yourself | Hire a designer or agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who plans the pages? | You or your team | Agreed in the scope |
| Who prepares the content? | Usually you, unless separately commissioned | May be included or supplied by you |
| Who tests the enquiry journey? | You must arrange it | Define the tests and acceptance criteria |
| Who makes later changes? | You, within the tool's capabilities | You, the supplier or both, by agreement |
| What determines flexibility? | Platform capabilities and your skills | Chosen platform, implementation and support |

A professional service does not automatically mean a bespoke codebase. A builder does not automatically mean poor performance. Assess the finished work and how it serves the customer.

## Include the cost of your own time

Compare setup, ongoing charges and the hours you will spend planning, building and maintaining the site. Include help you may need when something stops working.

A hypothetical 25-hour DIY project has an opportunity cost if those hours displace paid work. That does not make DIY the wrong choice; it makes your comparison more realistic. Our [website cost guide](/blog/small-business-website-cost-uk/) explains what else to include.

## Ask about control before you commit

Find out who controls the domain and accounts, what you can edit and which parts can be exported. Check cancellation and transfer terms, third-party subscriptions and what happens if the original supplier is unavailable.

For an existing site, confirm how useful content and URLs will be handled. A rebuild should have a reason beyond changing the technology. Start with the [website enquiry checks](/blog/website-not-generating-enquiries/) to identify the actual gap.

## Ask to see the working journey

Review examples on your phone. Find a service, understand the offer and try the relevant contact or booking flow. Ask the supplier to explain the decisions behind it and how the business maintains the site.
